Unit 5: Ordering food and drinks

A customer orders tea and food at a restaurant.

Register: Modern Standard Arabic. The dialogue is Modern Standard Arabic. Spoken dialect forms appear only in the labelled notes and are not quizzed.

Dialogue

  1. The waiterأَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا! مَاذَا تُرِيدُ؟
    ahlan wa-sahlan! mādhā turīdu?
    Welcome! What do you want?
  2. The customer (a man)أُرِيدُ شَايًا، مِنْ فَضْلِكَ.
    urīdu shāyan, min faḍlika.
    I want tea, please.
  3. The waiterهَلْ تُرِيدُ طَعَامًا؟
    hal turīdu ṭaʿāman?
    Do you want food?
  4. The customer (a man)نَعَمْ، أُرِيدُ دَجَاجًا وَخُبْزًا.
    naʿam, urīdu dajājan wa-khubzan.
    Yes, I want chicken and bread.
  5. The waiterحَسَنًا.
    ḥasanan.
    Very well.
  6. The customer (a man)شُكْرًا.
    shukran.
    Thank you.
  7. The waiterعَفْوًا.
    ʿafwan.
    You’re welcome.

Key vocabulary

  • يُرِيدُ
    yurīdu — to want (he wants)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Challenging · plain spelling يريد
    Past: أَرَادَ (arāda). I want: أُرِيدُ. The noun that follows takes -an: أُرِيدُ شَايًا “I want tea”.
  • شَاي
    shāy — tea
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling شاي
  • دَجَاج
    dajāj — chicken
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling دجاج
    A collective noun, used for the birds and the meat.
  • خُبْز
    khubz — bread
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling خبز
  • طَعَام
    ṭaʿām — food
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling طعام
  • مَاذَا
    mādhā — what (before a verb)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling ماذا
    Used before a verb: مَاذَا تُرِيدُ؟ “What do you want?”
  • هَلْ
    hal — (question word for yes/no questions)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling هل
    Put it at the start of a statement to ask a yes/no question: هَلْ تَعْمَلُ؟ “Do you work?”
  • مِنْ فَضْلِكَ
    min faḍlika — please (to a man)
    phrase · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling من فضلك
    To a woman: مِنْ فَضْلِكِ.
  • شُكْرًا
    shukran — thank you
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling شكرا
  • عَفْوًا
    ʿafwan — you’re welcome (also “excuse me”)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling عفوا
  • نَعَمْ
    naʿam — yes
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling نعم
  • حَسَنًا
    ḥasanan — OK, very well, fine
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling حسنا
    Said to agree or accept something. The -an ending (tanween fatha) is part of the standard form.

Spoken variants (not Modern Standard Arabic)

People often say it differently in speech. These are shown for recognition only and are not quizzed.

  • حَاضِر
    ḥāḍir — right away; of course (said by a waiter)
    Egyptian and Gulf Arabic · dialect
    In Modern Standard Arabic you can say حَسَنًا.
  • عَايِز شَاي
    ʿāyiz shāy — I want tea (said by a man)
    Egyptian Arabic · dialect
    Modern Standard Arabic: أُرِيدُ شَايًا.

Speaking practice

Listen to each line, then say it aloud two or three times.

  • أُرِيدُ شَايًا، مِنْ فَضْلِكَ.
    Line 2: مِنْ فَضْلِكَ: the ض (ḍ) is an emphatic d, heavier than the English d.
  • نَعَمْ، أُرِيدُ دَجَاجًا وَخُبْزًا.
    Line 4: دَجَاجًا: the ج is pronounced like the j in “jam” in Modern Standard Arabic.
  • أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا! مَاذَا تُرِيدُ؟
    Line 1: تُرِيدُ: the ر is a quick tap of the tongue.
