Unit 4: Asking someone’s name and nationality

Yusuf (from Egypt) and Layla (from Jordan) ask each other their names and where they are from.

Register: Modern Standard Arabic. The dialogue is Modern Standard Arabic. Spoken dialect forms appear only in the labelled notes and are not quizzed.

Dialogue

  1. Yusuf (a man)مَا اسْمُكِ؟
    mā smuki?
    What is your name? (to a woman)
  2. Layla (a woman)اِسْمِي لَيْلَى. وَمَا اسْمُكَ؟
    ismī laylā. wa-mā smuka?
    My name is Layla. And what is your name? (to a man)
  3. Yusuf (a man)اِسْمِي يُوسُف. مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتِ؟
    ismī yūsuf. min ayna anti?
    My name is Yusuf. Where are you from? (to a woman)
  4. Layla (a woman)أَنَا مِنَ الْأُرْدُنّ. أَنَا أُرْدُنِّيَّة. وَأَنْتَ؟
    anā mina l-urdunn. anā urdunniyya. wa-anta?
    I am from Jordan. I am Jordanian (a woman). And you?
  5. Yusuf (a man)أَنَا مِنْ مِصْر. أَنَا مِصْرِيّ.
    anā min miṣr. anā miṣriyy.
    I am from Egypt. I am Egyptian (a man).
  6. Layla (a woman)أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا!
    ahlan wa-sahlan!
    Welcome!

Key vocabulary

  • مَا اسْمُكَ؟
    mā smuka — what is your name? (to a man)
    phrase · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling ما اسمك
    To a woman: مَا اسْمُكِ؟
  • اِسْم
    ism — name
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling اسم
    The first letter is a connecting alif (hamzat wasl). “My name”: اِسْمِي.
  • مِنْ
    min — from
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling من
    Without harakat it is written exactly like مَنْ (“who”): only the vowel mark tells them apart.
  • أَيْنَ
    ayna — where
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling أين
  • الْأُرْدُنّ
    al-urdunn — Jordan
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling الأردن
    The name begins with الـ (“the”).
  • مِصْر
    miṣr — Egypt
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling مصر
  • أُرْدُنِّيّ
    urdunniyy — Jordanian (man)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Challenging · plain spelling أردني
    Woman: أُرْدُنِّيَّة (urdunniyya).
  • مِصْرِيّ
    miṣriyy — Egyptian (man)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling مصري
    Woman: مِصْرِيَّة (miṣriyya).
  • جِنْسِيَّة
    jinsiyya — nationality
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Medium · plain spelling جنسية
  • أَنْتِ
    anti — you (to a woman)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling أنت
    Only the last vowel differs from أَنْتَ.
  • أَنْتَ
    anta — you (to a man)
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling أنت
  • أَهْلًا
    ahlan — hi, welcome
    word · Modern Standard Arabic · Easy · plain spelling أهلا
    Often extended to أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا (welcome).

Spoken variants (not Modern Standard Arabic)

People often say it differently in speech. These are shown for recognition only and are not quizzed.

  • اِسْمَك إِيه؟
    ismak ēh? — what is your name? (to a man)
    Egyptian Arabic · dialect
    Modern Standard Arabic: مَا اسْمُكَ؟
  • شُو اسْمَك؟
    shū ismak? — what is your name? (to a man)
    Levantine Arabic · dialect
    Modern Standard Arabic: مَا اسْمُكَ؟
  • إِنْتَ مِنْ فِين؟
    inta min fīn? — where are you from? (to a man)
    Egyptian Arabic · dialect
    Modern Standard Arabic: مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟
  • مِنْ وَيْن إِنْتَ؟
    min wēn inta? — where are you from? (to a man)
    Levantine Arabic · dialect
    Modern Standard Arabic: مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟

Speaking practice

Listen to each line, then say it aloud two or three times.

  • مَا اسْمُكِ؟
    Line 1: اسْمُكِ: after مَا the first letter of اسْم is not pronounced separately, so the words run together: mā smuki.
  • أَنَا مِنَ الْأُرْدُنّ. أَنَا أُرْدُنِّيَّة. وَأَنْتَ؟
    Line 4: أُرْدُنِّيَّة: it has two shaddas (ن and ي). Hold each doubled sound briefly.
  • أَنَا مِنْ مِصْر. أَنَا مِصْرِيّ.
    Line 5: مِصْر: the ص (ṣ) is an emphatic s. Say it with the back of the tongue raised, which gives a darker, heavier sound than English s.
