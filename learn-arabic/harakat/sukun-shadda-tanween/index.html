Sukun, shadda and tanween

Three more marks finish the set: sukun means “no vowel here”, shadda doubles a consonant, and tanween adds a final -an, -in or -un to indefinite nouns.

  • بْ
    Sukun · سكون
    Sound: no vowel

    A small circle above the letter. The consonant is pronounced with NO vowel after it, joining directly to the next sound.

    مَكْتَبmaktab — office, desk

  • بَّ
    Shadda · شدّة
    Sound: doubled consonant

    A small w-like mark above the letter. The consonant is said twice (held longer). When a vowel mark is also present, it sits above or below the shadda.

    مُعَلِّمmuʿallim — teacher (male)

  • كِتَابٌ
    Tanween · تنوين
    Sound: -an, -in, -un

    A doubled vowel mark at the END of an indefinite noun, adding an “n”: ـً (-an), ـٍ (-in), ـٌ (-un). Fathatayn usually sits on an extra alif: ـًا. In everyday speech the final -n is often dropped at the end of a sentence.

    كِتَابًاkitaaban — a book
