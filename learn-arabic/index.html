تعلّم العربية

An interactive beginner course in Modern Standard Arabic: learn all 28 letters, how they connect, the short-vowel marks, and practice reading and typing. Free, no account, and your progress stays on your device.

What you will learn

The course follows a clear beginner path: 1. the 28 letters, their names, sounds and shapes; 2. harakat (fatha, kasra, damma, sukun, shadda, tanween); 3. practice quizzes; 4. reading and typing from single letters up to short sentences. Each letter lesson ends with a short check, and a lesson is marked complete only when you pass it.

How Arabic letters connect

Arabic is written right to left, and most letters join to the next one, changing shape by position: alone, at the beginning, in the middle, or at the end of a connected group. Six letters never join to the letter after them: ا د ذ ر ز و. After one of these the next letter starts a fresh shape, which is why words such as دار (daar, “house”) look broken into pieces.

Audio uses your device’s text-to-speech voice where available. It approximates pronunciation but is not a native recording, and transliteration is only a rough guide. Examples are Modern Standard Arabic; dialects pronounce some letters differently.
