## Points the author flagged for special attention

These are explanations that are simplified, disputed or depend on context. Please assess each one.

- **GR-ARTICLE-EXP-02** (gram-article, explanation): Says the first moon letter, alif, stands for words that begin with a hamza (for example الْأَبُ). Please confirm this is an acceptable way to present the 14 moon letters.
- **GR-ARTICLE-EXP-03** (gram-article, explanation): Teaching aid: the claim that all sun letters are made with the front of the tongue and that the lam “blends into” them. Please assess its accuracy and whether it suits beginners.
- **GR-ARTICLE-EXP-04** (gram-article, explanation): Silent alif of ال after another word (hamzat al-wasl): the elision rule, the pronunciation “fil-bayti”, and the course transliteration convention “l-”.
- **GR-ARTICLE-EX-09** (gram-article, example): اللَّيْلُ: the lam is itself a sun letter, so the doubled lam is said “allaylu”. Please confirm the description and the transliteration “al-laylu”.
- **GR-ARTICLE-EX-12** (gram-article, example): فِي الْبَيْتِ shows a kasra case ending before case endings are taught (lesson 8), and the note “said fil-bayti”. Please confirm.
- **GR-NOMINAL-EXP-02** (gram-nominal, explanation): Broad statement that an ordinary present-tense nominal sentence has no word for “am/is/are”. This is context dependent (other tenses use كَانَ, and a pronoun can appear between the two parts of some sentences). Please assess the level of simplification.
- **GR-NOMINAL-EXP-03** (gram-nominal, explanation): Case endings: the subject ends in a damma, and an indefinite predicate in tanween damma. Please confirm the endings shown.

Traditional grammar classes a sentence that begins with a pronoun or noun followed by a verb as nominal; the lessons use pronoun + verb sentences for clarity and teach verb-first sentences in lesson 7.
